The Full Process

An example workflow for this specific equipment setup. Your component manuals and equipment instructions control whenever they differ.

Read before handling components

This page is a checklist, not load data or a substitute for instruction. Use current published data for the exact bullet, powder, primer, and cartridge combination. If any value or procedure conflicts with a component or equipment manufacturer, stop and follow the manufacturer.

  • Wear eye protection. Work sober, rested, and free from interruptions, flame, sparks, and smoking.
  • Keep only one powder and one primer type on the bench. Verify every label before use; never substitute components casually.
  • Confirm whether every scale displays grains (gr) or grams (g). They are not interchangeable.
  • Begin at the published starting charge and follow the manual's work-up method. Stop for abnormal report, recoil, extraction, or any suspected obstruction.
  • Use an experienced mentor or qualified instructor for the first setup and loading session.

  1. Look at every case. Throw out any with cracks in the neck, splits on the body, or a bright ring near the base (that means the brass is about to split). Better to lose one case than ruin your rifle.

    Hook the probe over the inside wall and draw it from the base towards the mouth, turning the case as you go so you cover the whole circumference. Sound brass feels smooth and continuous. If the hook catches, dips, or rides over a ridge low down near the base, that case is thinning towards a separation — scrap it, whether or not you can see anything outside.

    Do this on brass you have fired several times, on any case whose history you do not know, and on anything that showed a suspicious shine in the visual check. Use a smooth wire and a light touch; you are feeling the wall, not scraping it.

    ⚠️ Never load or fire a case you suspect. If the head separates, the front of the case stays in the chamber and high-pressure gas vents back into the action. Scrap it, and crush or cut the case so it cannot find its way back into the pile.
    ✅ Good brass: smooth, no cracks, no bright rings near the base, and smooth and continuous to the probe inside.

  2. Do this before cleaning. Mount the decapping die in your press and run each case through to punch out the spent primer. With the pocket empty, the ultrasonic cleaner can reach inside and clean it — saving you the brush step.

    ✅ Deprime first = ultrasonic does all the cleaning, inside pocket and out.

  3. Run deprimed cases in your ultrasonic cleaner with diluted brass solution for 30–45 minutes. Because the primer pocket is already open, ultrasonic cleaning reaches inside and often reduces pocket brushing. Dry completely before moving on. Moisture near primers is dangerous.

    ✅ Cases bright inside and out. If any pockets still have black carbon, a quick 5-second brush fixes it.

  4. Pocket brush — inspect the pocket after cleaning and brush only as needed. Do not enlarge the pocket or remove brass.

    Flash hole deburring (optional) — do this only when your brass maker or documented process calls for it. Use a caliber-appropriate, depth-stopped tool exactly as its maker directs; uncontrolled cutting can alter the flash hole or case web. It is normally a one-time operation on suitable new brass.

    ✅ Clean and inspect first. Remove material only with the correct controlled tool and a documented reason.
    ⚠️ Military brass often has a crimped primer pocket — the crimp must be swaged or reamed out before you can seat a new primer. You'll feel it when the primer won't go in.

  5. Annealing is an optional, advanced operation used to control neck and shoulder hardness. Follow the annealer and brass manufacturer's validated setup for the exact machine, cartridge, and case lot. Only the neck and shoulder may be annealed; the case head must remain hard.

    ✅ Control the process by the equipment maker's prescribed time, power, temperature indicator, or calibration method — not appearance alone.
    ⚠️ Brass colour is not a reliable temperature gauge. Overheating, especially toward the case head, can permanently weaken the case. When the setup is uncertain, skip annealing and seek qualified instruction.

  6. Apply the sizing-lubricant maker's recommended thin, even film. Cover the case body and avoid excess on the shoulder; follow the die maker's direction for any neck lubrication. Never contaminate the case interior, primer pocket, primer, or powder.

    ⚠️ Excess lube can dent a shoulder; too little can stick a case. Remove all sizing lubricant after sizing and before priming or charging.

  7. This illustrated setup uses a body die, which sizes the case body and moves the shoulder while leaving the neck for a separate die. Establish a baseline from brass fired in the same rifle, then adjust and verify according to the die maker, firearm type, and published guidance.

    ⚠️ A body-die-plus-neck-die workflow is not universal. Semiautomatic, lever, pump, and hunting ammunition commonly needs full-length sizing and more chambering clearance than a precision bolt-action load. Confirm reliable chambering with inert components before loading a batch.
    • Slip comparator insert onto caliper jaw — .308 uses 0.330" datum hole
    • Measure case: base on fixed jaw, comparator seated on shoulder → write down reading
    • Run case through body die — adjust die in small increments
    • Measure again and compare the change — use the die maker's starting adjustment and the clearance appropriate to your rifle and action
    • Record the result; do not copy another rifle's raw comparator reading because comparator dimensions and chambers vary
    ✅ Use the smallest verified adjustment that still produces reliable chambering and extraction for the ammunition's intended rifle and use.

  9. In this two-die workflow, the bushing sizes the neck after the body die has already sized the body and shoulder. Select and install the bushing using the die maker's measurement method, accounting for actual loaded-neck diameter, brass thickness, and whether an expander is used.

    ✅ Verify grip with the exact case and bullet combination. Record the bushing, brass lot, loaded-neck diameter, and resulting performance.
    ⚠️ A bushing value or nominal “neck tension” copied from another load may be wrong for your brass. Too little grip can allow bullet movement; excessive sizing overworks brass.

  10. Set the trimmer to 2.005". Seat the case in the chuck, then advance the cutter shaft until the cutting edge just trims the case mouth. Stop when cutting ends, then verify length with calipers.

    ✅ After trimming, deburr and chamfer the case mouth — the next step.
    ⚠️ Do not trim below your manual's minimum case length. Over-trimming reduces neck grip and can affect consistency.

  11. After trimming, remove the outside burr and add only a light inside chamfer, following the tool maker's directions. Then remove all sizing lubricant and metal chips using a method compatible with the lubricant. Cases must be clean and completely dry before primers or powder are introduced.

    ✅ Before priming: mouth smooth, chips removed, primer pocket unobstructed, and no lubricant or moisture on or inside the case.

  12. Verify the exact primer type in the published load data, then use the priming tool as its maker directs. Seat each primer fully against the bottom of a clean pocket; it should finish below flush without crushing or flattening the cup. Measure only if your process and tool instructions specify a numeric depth.

    ⚠️ A high primer can cause ignition or function problems, especially in firearms with free-floating firing pins. Keep primers oriented correctly, never force one, and inspect every seated primer by sight and touch.
    ✅ Correct feel: smooth firm resistance, primer slightly recessed. Wrong feel: drops in with no resistance = discard that case.

  13. Confirm the published recipe and that the display is set to the intended unit, normally grains (gr), not grams (g). Enter the documented starting charge and operate the dispenser exactly as its manual directs. Independently verify its calibration and periodically compare charges with suitable check weights or a second trusted scale. Keep only one clearly identified powder on the bench.

    ⚠️ Before starting: run the scale's calibration routine using the check weights and procedure its manual specifies. Re-calibrate if you move the unit or bump the bench.
    ⚠️ After charging cases, look across all of them in the loading block. All powder levels should look the same height. An empty case or overfull case means a missed or double charge — stop and investigate every case.
    ⚠️ A case with NO powder looks like a normal empty case. Firing with no powder can lodge the bullet inside the barrel. If you then fire a second round — the barrel can rupture. If a round fires with a soft "pop" and no recoil, STOP. Do not fire again. Check the barrel is clear before continuing.
    ✅ Use the published starting load for the exact component combination and follow the source's work-up instructions. The container shown here is generic and is not a recommendation.

  14. Set the seating die using its instructions and the published cartridge overall length for the exact bullet SKU. Bullet profiles differ, so a length from another bullet of the same weight is not automatically valid. Verify the first cartridge, then check periodically and at the end of the batch.

    ✅ Record bullet SKU, seating setting, measured length, and whether the published recipe requires a crimp. Do not add a crimp by default.

  15. Use a cartridge gauge that is designed for loaded .308 Winchester ammunition and interpret it according to its instructions. A gauge result does not prove safe pressure or compatibility with every chamber. If a loaded round fails any dimensional or visual check, quarantine it and determine the cause.

    ⚠️ Do not run a primed or charged cartridge back through a sizing die or case trimmer. Disassemble rejected ammunition with an appropriate bullet puller before correcting brass preparation or seating. If checking fit in a firearm, follow the firearm maker's safe procedure with the firearm unloaded, ammunition source controlled, and muzzle in a safe direction.
    ⚠️ Inspect every primer, case, and bullet. Reject damage, contamination, a high or inverted primer, abnormal seating, or any cartridge that differs from the batch record.
    ✅ When done: label your box with caliber, bullet, powder, charge weight, primer, and date. Store in a cool dry place.

  16. Fired brass is the only feedback you get. Look at the heads before cleaning, while you still know which rounds they came from and the soot and marks are still readable.

    ⚠️ Pressure signs are late and unreliable. They vary with rifle, chamber, brass lot, primer, and temperature, and a load can be over pressure while showing none of them. Treat any sign only as a signal to stop — never as permission to keep increasing a charge, and never as a way to find your own maximum.

    Staying inside current published data, worked up the way the manual describes, is what actually keeps pressure in bounds. If you see any of these at or below a published charge, something else is wrong — a mis-weighed or double charge, the wrong powder, the wrong component, or an obstruction. Stop and account for it before you load or fire anything else.

    Signs to stop for
    What you see or feelWhat to do
    Unusual report or recoil, or any doubt about an obstructionStop immediately. Unload, and check the bore is clear before firing again.
    Bolt lifts hard, or the case will not extract normallyStop firing the batch. Do not fire the rest to see if it repeats.
    Bright ejector or extractor imprint on the headStop. Do not increase the charge.
    Primer flattened to the pocket edge, or flowed into the firing pin holeStop. Read alongside the other signs — primer appearance alone is a weak indicator.
    Primer pocket no longer grips a new primerRetire that case. Several in one batch is a warning about the load, not the brass.
    Bright ring around the body near the baseScrap the case — see Step 1. The brass is thinning towards a separation.
    ⚠️ If any sign appears, quarantine the rest of that batch rather than firing it off. Record what you saw against the batch record, and pull the remaining rounds down with a bullet puller.
    ✅ Write it down: rifle, charge, temperature, and how the brass looked. A batch record with results in it is what makes the next batch better.
